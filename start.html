The four-week Beginners course

Built for zero experience.

Three classes a week for four weeks. Every technique is taught from the ground up: how to fall safely, how to stand up, how to escape the positions you'll actually end up in, and the first chokes and joint locks. You drill with a partner at your level while a black or brown belt watches every rep.

  • Mon & Wed 8:00 pm, Thu 6:10 pm. Your free trial is any of those three slots. Miss a week? Pick it up on the next cycle.
  • Self-defense first: what to do if someone grabs you, tackles you or pins you.
  • No sparring until you want it. Then it's controlled, with people who know you're new.
  • After four weeks you join Fundamentals 1 and start earning stripes.

Why people stay

What you actually get.

Real self-defense

Jiu-Jitsu was built so a smaller person can control a bigger one. You learn to stay calm on the ground, get up, and get away. That confidence changes how you walk into a room.

Fitness that doesn't feel like a workout

An hour on the mat is strength, cardio and mobility at once. People lose weight here without ever counting anything, because they show up three times a week for the problem-solving, not the exercise.

A room that knows your name

You'll train with lawyers, nurses, actors, students and grandparents. The person who helps you with your first escape will be someone who was new six months ago.

Your first class, minute by minute

Nothing surprising.

Before

Book ahead so an instructor is ready for you, and sign the waiver online first; it creates your profile. Arrive 15 minutes early, rent a gi ($20, refunded when you sign up), meet the coach. Wear athletic clothes; bring water and flip-flops for the walk to the mat.

During

A short warm-up of movements you'll use every class, then two or three techniques shown slowly and drilled with a partner. The coach walks the mat and fixes details.

After

Watch the advanced students roll if you like, ask anything, and go home. If you want to keep going, we'll talk options then, not on the mat.

Questions

Ask us anything. Here are the usual ones.

How fit do I need to be?
You don't. The beginners course is paced for the room and you rest when you need to. Most people are surprised how much of the first month is learning to relax.
I'm over 40. Is that a problem?
No. A third of our adult students started after 40. Jiu-Jitsu rewards patience and technique over speed, which is why older beginners often progress faster than they expect.
Will I be paired with someone much bigger?
Coaches pair by size and experience, especially in the beginners course. You'll drill with someone you can learn from safely.
Do I need to buy a gi?
Not for the trial; rental is $20 and refunded when you sign up. If you join, you'll train in an academy gi (white or blue) with a rash guard underneath; we'll help you get sized.
What if I have an old injury?
Tell the coach before class. Almost everything can be adapted, and the beginners course avoids the positions that stress knees and shoulders until you're ready for them.
How often should I come?
Two or three times a week is the sweet spot for beginners. Once a week works; you'll just move a little slower.
How long until I get a blue belt?
Typically one and a half to two years of consistent training. Stripes come along the way, roughly every few months, so you always know where you stand.
